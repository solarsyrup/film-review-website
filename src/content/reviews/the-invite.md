I just finished watching *The Invite*, and I thought it was good. I found its premise genuinely interesting and pretty refreshing: a small cast of characters, a single location, and an entire story unfolding over the course of one evening. It’s a simple setup, but I think the film makes very good use of it.

I also thought the themes it explored were handled well, particularly superficiality, relationships, and the ways people navigate intimacy.

**Superficiality and Relationships**

One of the first themes I wanted to talk about is superficiality.

I feel like Angela, specifically, embodies a kind of superficiality that you often see in adults who are trying to be formal, polite, and normal. She’s concerned with maintaining appearances and keeping things conventional. Joe, on the other hand, feels much more natural. Whether that’s because of his contempt for his wife or simply because he wants to be honest with their guests, he’s less interested in keeping up appearances.

I also thought Hawk and Penia were interesting explorations of relationships, particularly what it means to recover from traumatic experiences and move forward with new people. They approach relationships more openly than Joe and Angela historically have, and the film explores different ways of understanding intimacy and being with another person.

I liked how these contrasting perspectives played off one another. The film isn’t necessarily presenting one approach as entirely right or wrong, but it gives you a lot to think about.

**Sexual Tension and Suspense**

A lot of the film’s drama and tension comes from sexual suspense, and I think this is handled really well.

One of my favorite parts is around the middle of the film, when it’s revealed that Hawk and Penia came over largely to see whether the four of them would be interested in sleeping together and potentially swapping partners.

I thought this reveal was executed really well. It changes the way you look at the interactions that came before it, and it gives the rest of the evening a completely different atmosphere.

I also thought the film’s conversations about sex felt very natural. Obviously, I can’t speak from personal experience when it comes to having these kinds of conversations, but they felt believable and weren’t handled in an unnecessarily awkward or artificial way.

**Performances and Direction**

I thought all the actors did a great job, especially Seth Rogen.

He’s a surprisingly versatile actor, and this film reminded me of that. His laugh is iconic, of course, but you can put him in a children’s movie, a comedy, or something like this, and he can make it work. I think it’s easy to forget just how much range he has.

I also thought Olivia Wilde was great, both as an actor and as the film’s director. She does a really good job bringing everything together, and I particularly liked how the performances complemented the intimate setting.

**Minor Criticisms**

My main criticism is that I think the characters were a little undercooked.

I wanted to know more about their backgrounds and what made them who they are. For example, we don’t learn all that much about Joe’s music career, and I felt like there were other aspects of the characters that could have been explored more deeply.

It’s not that the film doesn’t go beyond the surface level. It does, and there are some interesting layers to the characters. I just wish it had gone a little further. I think there’s more to explore with all four of them, especially Penia, who I found particularly interesting.

I also thought the ending was a little short. I liked it, and I think it implied quite a lot without needing to spell everything out, but I wouldn’t have minded spending a little more time with these characters before the credits rolled.

**Final Thoughts**

Overall, I liked *The Invite* quite a bit.

I thought the decision to have the entire film take place in one location, over the course of a single evening, was a great choice. Even having the daughter away for the night contributes to that contained feeling and allows the film to focus almost entirely on these four people and their interactions.

Its exploration of relationships, superficiality, intimacy, and the expectations people place on one another made for an interesting watch. The performances were great, the tension was handled very well, and while I wish the characters had been developed a little further, I still came away satisfied.

A good, refreshing film with plenty to think about.
