Recently, I've been seeing the Rick and Morty Szechuan sauce clip from *Eighth Grade*, directed by Bo Burnham. I knew Bo Burnham as the "Welcome to the Internet" guy, and the scene was so awkward that it made me intrigued about the movie—or the film, if you will.

## Plot and such

The movie is about Kayla Day, a socially awkward eighth grader navigating the struggles of her last week of middle school. She's invited to a girl's birthday party, has a crush on this dumbass kid whose name I can't remember, and generally struggles to fit in.

She also shadows at a high school, where she meets a girl named Olivia and her friends. They don't seem like they'd necessarily be a negative influence on her, and Olivia invites Kayla to hang out with them. However, on the way home, one of the boys, Riley, makes an unwanted sexual advance toward Kayla and pressures her into an uncomfortable situation.

This brings me to Kayla's father, Mark Day, whom I really enjoyed in the movie. I think that when I'm a father, I hope to be like him. He was a really realistic portrayal of a father, and I don't think he could have done much to prevent what happened with Riley.

Earlier, he had been following Kayla because he was worried about her, but she caught him. He was just concerned about her making friends and wanted to make sure she was okay. I feel like if my daughter came home and told me she'd made friends, especially if I knew she'd been struggling socially, I'd be happy for her and give her some independence. I wouldn't want to constantly interfere.

One thing I thought about, although the movie didn't really touch upon it much, was how Riley's behavior would be received nowadays. I feel like there might have been more of an "atta boy" attitude toward what he almost did to Kayla, whereas nowadays I think he'd be called disgusting for it. I don't know if it would actually be different, but I feel like there's a greater emphasis on condemning people who sexually assault or harass others.

## Thoughts

I thought Gabe, the kid from the Rick and Morty Szechuan sauce clip, would be a much bigger character. I anticipated the movie going in a direction where Kayla meets Gabe at the pool party, the two learn that they can be themselves around each other, and they grow together and become less socially awkward.

I wasn't disappointed that this didn't happen, though. I really loved his appearances, and I think he was the perfect counterbalance to Kayla's awkwardness.

Gabe seemed either completely unaware or overly aware of his own awkwardness, and I thought that was a perfect contrast to Kayla, who falls somewhere in the middle. He was such an interesting character to watch, especially because his awkwardness felt so different from hers.

The movie perfectly harnesses awkwardness in so many of its scenes and paints a really realistic picture of what the middle school experience is like.

While I was watching, I kept thinking about how I could never be a middle school teacher. When I was in middle school, I thought all the kids were stupid, and watching this movie reminded me of that experience.

I think Bo Burnham did a great job portraying that period of life. The characters felt realistic, their interactions were believable, and the awkwardness never seemed particularly exaggerated just for the sake of comedy.

Overall, I really enjoyed *Eighth Grade*. I think Burnham captured the middle school experience remarkably well, especially the social struggles, awkward interactions, and relationships between the characters.
