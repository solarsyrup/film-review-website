Recently, I’ve been seeing the Rick and Morty Szechuan sauce clip from Eighth Grade on TikTok and instagram reels. This film is directed by Bo Burnham, who I knew as the “Welcome to the Internet” guy, and the scene was so awkward and funny it made me intrigued about the film, so today I decided to give it a watch.

## Plot and such

The movie is about Kayla Day, a socially awkward eighth grader navigating the struggles of her last week of middle school. She’s invited to a girl’s birthday party, has a crush on this dumbass kid whose name I can’t remember, and generally struggles to fit in.

She also shadows at a high school, where she meets a girl named Olivia and her friends. They don’t seem like they’d necessarily be a negative influence on her, and Olivia invites Kayla to hang out with them. However, on the way home, one of the boys, Riley, makes an unwanted sexual advance toward Kayla and pressures her into an uncomfortable situation.

This brings me to Kayla’s father, Mark Day, whom I really enjoyed in the movie. I think that when I’m a father, I hope to be like him. He was a really realistic portrayal of fatherhood, and I don’t think he could have done much to prevent what happened with Riley.

Earlier, he had been tailing Kayla because he was worried about her, but she caught him. He was just concerned about her making friends and wanted to make sure she was okay. I feel like if my daughter came home and told me she’d made friends, especially if I knew she’d been struggling socially, I’d be happy for her and give her some independence. I wouldn’t want to constantly interfere.

One thing I thought about, although the movie didn’t really touch upon it much, was how Riley’s behavior would be received nowadays. I feel like there might have been more of an almost “atta boy” attitude toward what he almost did to Kayla,  to the men at least, whereas nowadays I think he’d be made fun of and called disgusting for it. I don’t know if it would actually be different, but I feel like there’s a greater emphasis on condemning people who sexually assault or harass others now compared to then.

## Thoughts

I thought Gabe, the kid from the Rick and Morty Szechuan sauce clip, would be a much bigger character. I anticipated the movie going in a direction where Kayla meets Gabe at the pool party, the two learn that they can be themselves around each other, and they grow together and become less socially awkward.

I wasn’t disappointed that this didn’t happen, though. I really loved his relatively few appearances, and I think he was the perfect counterbalance to Kayla’s awkwardness.

Gabe seemed either completely unaware or overly aware of his own awkwardness, and I thought that was a perfect contrast to Kayla, who falls somewhere in the middle. He was such an interesting character to watch, especially because his awkwardness felt so different from hers.

Something else I really enjoyed was the depiction of social media, phones, and technology. I thought it was really realistic, especially in how it seemed to make Kayla’s life worse while simultaneously being one of the few ways she could socially interact with her peers. Even online, though, she struggles to find her personality and figure out how to present herself to other people.

Her online videos were a particularly interesting way of illustrating this. Kayla is almost an unreliable narrator in her own videos. She’s essentially LARPing as a socially capable person, giving advice about confidence and interacting with people when she herself struggles with those exact things. I think it’s a way for her to deal with the fact that she isn’t necessarily the person she wants to be. She can present herself as confident online, even if she doesn’t feel that way in real life.

Another thing I noticed was how dated the movie felt. It came out in 2018, and there are tons of references to the pop culture and technology of that period. I don’t think that’s necessarily a bad thing, though. If anything, it makes the movie feel like a time capsule, another prominent symbol in the film.

I would have sold my soul to go to high school during this time. I know that’s probably just nostalgia speaking, because I was younger then, but there’s something about the culture of that period that I really like. All those references make the movie feel incredibly accurate to its time, and I think they’ll only become more interesting as the years go by.

The movie perfectly harnesses awkwardness in so many of its scenes and paints a really realistic picture of what the middle school experience is like.

While I was watching, I kept thinking about how I could never be a middle school teacher. When I was in middle school, I thought all the kids were stupid, and watching this movie reminded me of that experience.

I think Bo Burnham did a great job portraying that period of life. The characters felt realistic, their interactions were believable, and the awkwardness never seemed particularly exaggerated just for the sake of comedy.

Overall, I really enjoyed Eighth Grade. I think Burnham captured the middle school experience remarkably well, especially the social struggles, awkward interactions, and relationships between the characters.
