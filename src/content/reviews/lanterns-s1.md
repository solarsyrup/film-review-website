I just finished watching Lanterns. I know I’m a little late to it, and I wasn’t watching every Sunday when the episodes were coming out, but a friend whom I regularly see movies with introduced me to the show. He told me it was really good, so I gave it a watch, and I gotta say, I loved it.

It took me a while to get through, though, because each episode was upwards of 55 minutes long. I think the finale even reached over an hour. I’m used to shows like Breaking Bad, which have relatively long episodes, but once we get past the hour mark, it’s like, wow, this is practically a movie every episode.

## Plot and such

The show primarily takes place in Rushville, Nebraska, which I have learned is actually a real town. It follows Green Lanterns Hal Jordan and John Stewart as they investigate a series of mysteries involving the Manhunters on Earth.

There are several conflicts throughout the story, including trying to find a Manhunter on Earth, figuring out who killed Hal in the second-to-last episode, and discovering the identity of the next Manhunter.

The story isn’t exclusively set in Rushville, though. We visit another planet, and John has his own things going on back home.

There are also several jumps between 2016, when much of the story originally takes place, and 2026, the present day. John has grown a lot as a person since then, and he’s given up his right to be a Green Lantern following the events of 2016, when everything went horribly wrong.

## Thoughts

I thought the show was great. I’ve seen people online complaining that there weren’t enough Green Lantern constructs, that there weren’t any massive space battles like they were expecting, or that the show was too grounded in reality.

Apparently, because they’re superheroes, they have to use their superpowers all the time. I don’t really get that criticism.

Personally, I loved the buddy-cop storyline. I don’t think a Green Lantern show needs constant space fights and elaborate constructs to be interesting. I enjoyed the more grounded approach, and I think it worked really well for the story they were trying to tell.

The score was great, and I loved the music they chose for the needle drops. The acting, cast, writing, and story were all excellent. I particularly liked how clear the characters’ motivations were. I understood why they were doing what they were doing, which made the story a lot easier to get invested in.

As many have pointed out, the dynamic between Hal Jordan and John Stewart was really good. It was almost a father and son story, and dealt with the feelings of obsolescence, fear, and the nature of people.

One thing I didn’t like quite as much was how the events of 2016 were explained. We’re told that everything went wrong and that a lot of innocent people died, but I was a little confused about how that actually happened. I don’t remember Hal Jordan directly killing anybody. I assume it had something to do with the satellite from the Macon family, but I’m not entirely sure. Maybe I misunderstood something, but I wish that part had been clearer. Or I simply forgot, because I didn’t watch the whole episode in one sitting.

Beyond the show itself, I think Lanterns fits really well into the broader DCU. After seeing Clayface a couple of weeks ago and Superman last year, I’m starting to think this new universe has a lot of potential.

I haven’t gotten around to Peacemaker season two yet, although I plan to watch it soon. Aside from Supergirl, though, the DCU seems to be shaping up really well, especially compared to the DCEU that came before it.

I really didn’t like much of the old DCEU. I don’t know whether that was Zack Snyder’s fault or something else, but it wasn’t working for me. James Gunn, on the other hand, seems to be doing a really good job with the projects he’s directly involved with. I noticed that he was also an executive producer on Lanterns.

Overall, I loved Lanterns. I thought the story was great, the characters were compelling, and the more grounded buddy-cop approach was exactly what made it interesting to me. I really don’t understand some of the complaints about it not being superhero-y enough. Not every superhero story needs to be a massive spectacle, and I think Lanterns is a great example of that.
