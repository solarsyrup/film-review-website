I’ve just finished my first rewatch of *Common Side Effects*, and it was really good. It was great. It was excellent. It was perfect. I’ll just say it: **it’s perfect.** I know that sounds bold, but you’ll come to understand why I believe that.

**How I First Watched It**

The first time I watched *Common Side Effects* was at my good friend Ryan’s house. Me, him, and a few of our friends went over, and his parents weren’t home. This must have been at the very tail end of freshman year. His sister, who was a senior, had a bunch of her friends over, and when we all walked into the house, it was clear they were having your traditional high school party.

They were watching *Final Destination* or something, and we just kind of took over their TV and started watching whatever. Meanwhile, all these seniors are just doing random stuff. I remember they smashed through a screen door or something and jumped into the freezing-cold pool.

At one point, one of the guys was like, “Yo, you guys want anything from the store?” And we were like, “Uh, yeah, can you go get us a pack of Dr Pepper?” And he brought it back. I thought that was pretty cool. I want to be like him.

Anyway, we slowly kicked them off the TV from watching *Final Destination* and ended up watching probably *Rick and Morty* and some other adult animated shows. Eventually, I forget whether it was my friend Teo or Ryan, but one of them put on *Common Side Effects*.

And even on my first watch, I thought it was really good.

**The Plot and Premise**

I think the premise of the show is amazing. It’s essentially a thought experiment: what if you had a pharmaceutical medicine that could heal anything? And I feel like the show’s portrayal of what would happen in that scenario is pretty similar to what would actually happen if it were real. You’d have Big Pharma and everybody else hunting you down.

Having the government, the DEA, the FBI, and all these other three-letter agencies involved, as well as private mercenaries, makes the whole thing feel grounded in reality. And then there’s Jonas Backstein who serves on the boards of multiple huge companies. The twist of irony in him getting lung cancer and frantically searching for the very drug he sought to destroy is just so good. It’s so good.

There are so many moments like that throughout the show where you’re just left thinking, “Wow”. I think the creators did an amazing job with the writing. It’s the small, tiny details that make it great.

**Characters, Animation, and Symbolism**

The show’s humor is great. The characters are all great. Their motivations are there. The animation is great. The song choices are great. The voices are great. I loved Mike Judge specifically as Rick Kruger, and I love the art style.

Harrington and Copano are amazing characters. I love the dynamic between them, and also the dynamic between Marshall and Frances.

There’s all sorts of symbolism throughout the show, too. Like the cardinal on Sonia’s postcard and then the cardinal in the tree before she died. Or the Rubik’s Cube on Frances’s desk with Cecily. Those are just a few examples, and there are tons more. They’re just some of the smaller things I picked up on.

And since this was my second time watching it, I was able to pick up on some more things that I hadn’t noticed the first time around.

**The Ending and My Final Thoughts**

I’ve also heard some complaints about the ending being too ambiguous. People are left wondering what’s going to happen next, and I can understand that criticism to an extent. For a while, it made sense. But they announced that Season 2 would be coming out in January of this year, so there will be a continuation of the story. It doesn’t just end there, which I’m quite excited for.

Oh yeah, back to the story about Ryan’s house. We actually watched most of the show in one sitting, which was crazy. We got up to around Episode 8, and then I watched Episodes 9 and 10 at home on my own because I was like, “Well shit, I want to see where this goes.”

I really do think this is a perfect show. There’s nothing I can point to that I genuinely dislike. Everything’s great. It’s grounded, it has a good concept, and everything about it is awesome.
