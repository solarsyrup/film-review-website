Last night, or technically early this morning, I watched *Baby Driver*. I’d heard of it before, but what actually convinced me to watch it was, of all things, my physics class.

A couple of weeks ago, *Baby Driver* came up in one of our physics problems. We were learning about circular and centripetal motion, and my teacher showed us a clip from the laundromat scene to demonstrate it. I thought it looked good, and I figured, well shit, if my physics teacher who I respect very much likes it, maybe I will too.

So I finally watched it, and it was really, really, really good. I only have a few very minor complaints.

## Circles, Motion, and Cinematography

One thing I absolutely loved about this film was its use of circles.

There’s one scene in particular where Debora and Baby are having dinner. The camera circles around them while the music plays and they’re being served. The plates are circles. The cups are circles. The camera itself is rotating around them. It’s so good.

It actually reminded me of the *JoJo’s Bizarre Adventure: Steel Ball Run* intro, with all its imagery of spinning and circles, because that’s essentially what *Steel Ball Run* is about: the Spin.

And in a way, I think *Baby Driver* is about circular motion too. Whether it’s the movement of the cars, the camera, or even the characters themselves, everything seems to move in circles. There’s this constant sense of rotation, of things coming back around. I don’t know if I’m explaining this particularly well, but it’s something I kept noticing, and I loved it.

The cinematography is excellent in general. Another scene that really stood out to me was the long, continuous tracking shot of Baby walking through the street to get coffee. The song playing is *Harlem Shuffle* by Bob & Earl, and as Baby walks, the lyrics appear throughout the environment, written on walls, signs, and other objects around him.

It’s such a creative sequence. I love when a film pays that much attention to the relationship between its music and visuals.

## Characters and Performances

I thought the characters were great. I particularly liked Doc, and I also really liked Joseph, the elderly deaf man Baby lives with. Joseph was amazing, and I loved getting to learn more about him and his relationship with Baby.

Another little detail I loved was Baby’s hobby of making music out of recordings of people’s voices. Everyone was skeptical that he actually did that, rather than just recording their conversations for each heist. I thought it was such a fun and interesting part of his character.

Baby's tinnitus was an interesting plot device; it bundles making the background music diegetic and incorporating his parents' death and love of music all into one is an amazing choice. I’ve heard some complaints about the acting, but I personally didn’t have a problem with it. I thought the performances were very good.

## The Women in the Film

Another criticism I’ve heard concerns the way the women are written, particularly Darling and Debora, since they’re the two main female characters with speaking roles.

And I can see where that criticism comes from.

I don’t think the movie passes the Bechdel test, and both characters are written in ways where their stories depend heavily on the men around them. I’ve even heard people argue that if you’re going to write women like that, you might as well not write them at all.

But I don’t entirely agree.

Even if Debora and Darling are written that way, I think their relationships and decisions are important to how the plot develops. For example, when Baby tells Debora that they’re going to leave together at 2 a.m., she waits for him.

If Debora had been written as a more independent character who simply decided to leave without him, that entire part of the story wouldn’t have happened. But she loves Baby, and I think that’s a major part of both her character and his.

I understand the criticism, but I also think the way these characters act serves a purpose within the story.

## Final Thoughts

*Baby Driver* is a beautiful film.

The cinematography is excellent, the characters are memorable, the performances are great, and the attention to detail is incredible. I especially love how the film incorporates music and movement into practically everything it does.

The use of circles and rotation is something I’m going to remember for a long time. It’s not something I expected to notice when I started watching, but it ended up being one of my favorite aspects of the film.

Very beautiful, amazing film.

**★★★★★ — 5/5**
