# 21 Jump Street (2012)

Today I watched *21 Jump Street* for the first time. One of my friends had referenced it before, especially the famous “Fuck you, Doug!” scene with the guy who turns out to be the drug supplier at the end of the movie. Between my friends referencing it and the movie being pretty well-liked overall, I figured I'd finally give it a watch.

And I enjoyed it! It was a really funny movie. Very funny, actually. Jonah Hill and Channing Tatum's dynamic was especially good, and it offered a lot of opportunities for the two of them to get into ridiculous situations and wacky shenanigans.

But I don't know. I think the concept needed some work.

## The Premise

The basic concept is that two guys who went to high school together, one a nerdy loser and the other a popular kid, eventually become police officers. They get assigned to go undercover at a high school to investigate a drug operation.

But when they infiltrate the school, their roles are essentially reversed. Jonah Hill's character, who was a loser in high school, suddenly becomes popular, while Channing Tatum's character, who used to be the popular guy, finds himself hanging out with the nerds.

I think this concept could have worked a lot better if the movie weren't so insistent on the idea that high school culture had completely changed in just seven years.

Apparently, seven years ago, caring about the environment, studying, being gay, or even wearing both straps of your backpack wasn't cool. But now, suddenly, all of those things are cool, and you are cool for doing them.

I just don't see high school culture changing *that* drastically in seven years. I understand that it's exaggerated for comedic effect, but I feel like the premise relies a little too heavily on that exaggeration.

## Characters and Comedy

That being said, the movie is genuinely funny.

The dynamic between Jonah Hill and Channing Tatum really carries it. They work well together, and the reversal of their high school social standings creates some pretty entertaining situations.

I particularly liked Channing Tatum's character's new friend group of nerds. Especially the Dax Flame who's now become a TikToker. I thought it was pretty cool how those guys ended up being genuinely likable characters rather than just the stereotypical nerds you'd expect to see out of a movie like this.

I also liked the little subplot involving Channing Tatum's character and his chemistry teacher, who develops an attraction to him. That led to a couple of funny moments that I enjoyed.

There's a lot of good comedy in the movie, and I definitely found myself laughing throughout it.

## Minor Problems with the Story

One thing that bothered me was the situation with Molly.

She's an eighteen-year-old high school student who ends up going to prom with a man who's around twenty-five years old. And the movie never really addresses how fucked up that is.

They even kiss afterward!

I know it's a comedy, and I'm not expecting every situation to be treated with complete seriousness, but it still felt like something that should have been acknowledged.

There were also a few things that I felt weren't entirely resolved by the end. Nothing that completely ruined the movie for me, but enough that I walked away feeling a little unsatisfied.

## Final Thoughts

Overall, *21 Jump Street* was a funny movie with some genuinely great moments.

I liked Jonah Hill and Channing Tatum's chemistry, the ridiculous situations they got themselves into, and some of the smaller character interactions. But I think the central premise could have been handled better, and certain parts of the story left me wanting a little more.

I enjoyed it, just not quite as much as I expected to.

I'll probably give *22 Jump Street* a try sometime soon and see how it compares.
