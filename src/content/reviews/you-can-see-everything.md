Right after Clayface, which hardly had anybody seated, I took a trip to the bathroom, and upon entering my theater of choice, I see a sea of people waiting for the start of You Can See Everything. I try to find my friend, and after a minute see him at the very top of the stairs waving at me. Right after I am seated, the A24 logo appears and the film begins.

## Recap

It is difficult to explain what exactly this film is. Directed by Nathan Fielder, it is about Elizabeth Holmes in the month or so prior to her imprisonment. She invites Fielder and company to her house where he documents her family's life. Consisting of Holmes herself, Billy (her boyfriend) and two children, the family seems functional. I love Fielder's deadpan and inquisitive nature. He doesn't care about asking questions to glean the information he needs. 

A favorite scene of mine from this first half is when Fielder shows Holmes the ["Who's On First"](https://youtu.be/RlllabwmWcc) skit from Abbott and Costello and asks her if she finds it funny. She finds it funny but doesn't understand the joke, and Fielder determines that she doesn't understand the wordplay, and thus doesn't understand why what she said in an interview could be interpreted differently.

The second half of the movie takes place in late 2025 and early 2026, in the months up to the documentary's release. Fielder tries to arrange a meeting with Holmes without the involvement of Billy, who took over his lover's Twitter and is seeking to make a profit from their involvement in the film. It features reenactments from Amanda Seyfried who also played Holmes in the television mini-series *The Dropout (2022)*.

## Thoughts

I adored the dynamics between characters, especially the indirect rivalry between Seyfried and Holmes, the latter of which is bullied in prison for Holmes' portrayal in Seyfried's show which the other inmates took for reality. I liked the ambiguousness of Billy's relationship with Holmes. Both claim they love each other more than life itself, but Billy seems to manipulate Holmes' decisions for his personal gain. Holmes claims she supports any decision she makes, despite previous words suggesting otherwise.

Some notable themes include the misrepresentation of people in media, manipulation, the thin veil between the truth and falsehood. It really is a hard film to describe, and I recommend anybody reading this to go see it while it's still in theaters.
