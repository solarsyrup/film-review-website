I went to a funeral today. It’s the first one I’ve been to as a fully conscious person. I went to my great-grandfather’s when I was nine, but I don’t remember enough to say I was conscious. I do remember playing Subway Surfers with my cousins while the cadaver of our great-grandfather, who lived in Poland through World War II, lied in the front.

The funeral today however was different. It wasn’t an almost ninety year old man. It was someone just a year older than me.

I didn’t know the person who died; he went to my middle school, the grade above me. He played soccer with a few friends of mine. He was the cousin of another friend of mine, who was his best friend. A few weeks ago he committed suicide.

 It felt wrong being there, knowing I didn’t personally know the dead. However, I feel like it was the right thing to do. I met his parents and little sister, who was a friend of my sister. I gave his dad a handshake, and his mom a hug. I went over to my friend, grieving his best friend, and gave him a little hug. 

I’ve never really fully understood the saying “showing up matters” until today. Like, obviously it does for work, school or other events. But I think my presence was appreciated, although I spent no more than fifteen minutes in the funeral home. I realized that my showing up mattered to everybody there; not just to the mourning family, but to the community gathered in remembrance.

I would say this is the first time I’ve formally been introduced to Death (hence the title, Death Part I). While we’ve been face to face a few times while I was young, this is our introduction and first true encounter. To see someone so close to me in age be taken is a bizarre experience. This is only the beginning of me and Death’s relationship.
